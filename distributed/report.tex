\section{The LASSQD library}
\label{sec:library}

LASSQD uses sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) to solve the fragments
of a localized active space (LAS) calculation. Quantum circuits provide samples;
classical fragment solves return reduced density matrices (RDMs), which mrh's
RDM-based LASSCF uses to update the orbitals.

The library consists of stage functions that map records to records. One
macrocycle has four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Capture.} \code{capture_embedding} enters mrh with temporary
  callbacks that record every fragment's Hamiltonian at the orbitals and
  starting RDMs of a \code{LASState}, then stops mrh before anything is solved.
  It also computes each fragment's ROHF sampling basis and the integrals in that
  basis, once. The first macrocycle starts from \code{initial_state}, mrh's
  initial-guess RDMs.
  \item \textbf{Sample.} \code{build_circuits} calls a caller-supplied builder
  for each fragment and groups the circuits, gluing the fragments of a group
  into one circuit. \code{submit_circuits} submits all or some circuits in one
  sampler job, and \code{collect_samples} splits the pub results, possibly from
  several jobs, into per-fragment counts.
  \item \textbf{Solve.} \code{solve_fragments} runs \code{FragmentSQD} on each
  fragment outside mrh, independently of the other fragments: configuration
  recovery and batched selected-CI solves under a subspace policy, returning
  the energy and RDMs of the lowest-energy result that the policy reports
  across recovery iterations.
  \item \textbf{Update orbitals.} \code{update_orbitals} checks that each result
  was solved for the captured fragment problem, then re-enters mrh at the same
  starting RDMs with callbacks that return the stored results. mrh computes the
  coupled LAS energy and takes one orbital macrostep.
\end{enumerate}

The stages exchange records: \code{LASState} (orbitals and fragment RDMs),
\code{Embedding} and \code{Fragment} (the captured fragment problems with their
sampling bases), \code{FragmentCircuits} (the compiled circuits, stored as QPY,
and the register holding each fragment's measurements), \code{FragmentSamples}
(counts and the embedding they were sampled from), \code{FragmentResult} (one
fragment's energy, LAS-basis RDMs and best SCI state), and \code{SQDState} (a
fragment solver's random-generator state, carryover strings and their orbital
basis). Every record is saved to and loaded from HDF5 with a format version.
The records module imports only NumPy and h5py; \code{FragmentCircuits} and
\code{collect_samples} also need Qiskit. A QPU gateway therefore needs neither
mrh, PySCF nor qiskit-addon-sqd. The library's tests check that restoring the
saved records reproduces the uninterrupted calculation bit for bit.

Every process that calls mrh builds the LAS object with \code{lassqd.LASSCF},
mrh's RDM-based LASSCF factory, which applies density fitting when the mean
field uses it. The mrh-facing stages reject a LAS object that would mix exact
active-space integrals with density-fitted potentials.

\code{run_lassqd} composes the stages serially in one process and repeats them
until the absolute total-energy change is below the tolerance or the cycle
limit is reached. The distributed workflow below composes the same stages
across processes and machines.

\textbf{Trim SQD.} This design uses trim SQD, a subspace policy added in
qiskit-addon-sqd 0.14 as \code{TrimPolicy} in \code{qiskit_addon_sqd.trim} and
demonstrated in \path{docs/lassqd.ipynb}. \code{FragmentSQD} passes the policy to
SQD as its \code{policy} option. Each recovery iteration then has two rounds of
diagonalization:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Screening.} SQD draws one pool of
  $\code{samples_per_batch} \times B$ bitstrings and deals it into $B =
  \code{num_batches}$ disjoint batches. Each batch is diagonalized and trimmed
  to the fraction \code{trim_ratio} of its CI strings, per spin sector, that
  carry the most weight, at most \code{max_strings_per_trim} of them.
  \item \emph{Merged.} The survivors of all batches are merged, keeping each
  batch's ranking, into one subspace, which is bounded by \code{max_dim} and
  diagonalized once more.
\end{enumerate}
The merged diagonalization is the result that the iteration reports. Its
energy and occupancies drive convergence and the next recovery. Its
highest-weight strings, selected by \code{carryover_threshold},
\code{carryover_ratio} and \code{max_carryover}, seed the next iteration and,
through \code{SQDState}, the next macrocycle. An iteration therefore costs
$B + 1$ diagonalizations. The notebook's configuration, used as the reference
throughout, is \code{num_batches=5}, \code{samples_per_batch=100},
\code{max_iterations=2}, \code{max_dim=150},
\code{TrimPolicy(trim_ratio=0.7, carryover_ratio=1.0)} and
\code{symmetrize_spin=True}. At the same \code{max_dim}, trim SQD there ends
about 2\,mHartree from classical LASSCF, against 16--22\,mHartree for the
default policy. Screening helps only when the sampled configurations greatly
outnumber what one diagonalization can hold, so compare against
\code{num_batches=1} on each new system. The default \code{StandardPolicy}
remains available. The resource estimates below state where it differs.

\section{The proposed LASSQD--QCSC Prefect distributed workflow}

The design uses \textbf{mrh unchanged}. LASSQD captures the fragment
Hamiltonians, solves the fragments outside mrh, then re-enters mrh with
callbacks that return the completed results; \code{capture_embedding} and
\code{update_orbitals} make the two mrh calls. Prefect coordinates dependencies;
QCSC launches HPC jobs and handles QPU submission and fetching.

The selected scope is several fragments, with each diagonalization, of a
screening batch or of the merged subspace, fitting on one node. For fragment
$f$ with $B_f$ batches, launch one MPI job on $P_f \le B_f$ nodes, with one
rank per node. Rank $r$ solves the screening batches $b \equiv r
\pmod{P_f}$. The default $P_f = B_f$ gives one batch per rank. Retain that
allocation across recovery iterations. Different fragments use separate MPI
jobs and can run concurrently. Use the existing collective
\code{diagonalize_fermionic_hamiltonian} interface with
\code{policy=TrimPolicy(...)} and an application-supplied \code{sci_solver};
SQD's recovery loop and the policy remain unchanged. The solver is collective
and is called twice per iteration: once with the $B_f$ screening batches and
once with the single merged subspace.

The Prefect coordinator exchanges storage references. Full numerical arrays
and CI vectors remain on HPC, including MPI transfers between the ranks of
each fragment job. The control rank must accommodate the merged
diagonalization and the gathered screening results. With the local trim
described below, the screening results are CI strings, not states.
Section~\ref{sec:resources} quantifies both requirements.

Figure~\ref{fig:distributed-workflow} shows the complete workflow and the
execution nodes. It distinguishes the two loops. The \textcolor{accent}{%
\textbf{configuration-recovery loop}} runs inside each fragment's MPI job and
determines most of the HPC cost; the \textbf{macrocycle loop} returns to
Hamiltonian capture after each orbital update and is coordinated by Prefect.
The recovery loop belongs to the fragment solve rather than to the capture
stage: capture runs once per macrocycle, before sampling, whereas every
recovery iteration needs that fragment's counts and the previous iteration's
occupancies. Node labels denote resource roles: short HPC stages may reuse
nodes, and the coordinator, scheduler submitter, and QPU gateway may share a
service host.

\textbf{Capture a fixed embedding.}
An HPC task loads a committed \code{LASState} snapshot of orbitals and fragment
RDMs and calls \code{capture_embedding}, which passes those RDMs explicitly to
mrh and captures all fragment Hamiltonians through temporary callbacks. mrh
constructs their effective one-electron Hamiltonians before its fragment loop,
so the captured problems can be solved concurrently without changing that
dependency structure. The same task computes each fragment's ROHF sampling basis
and stores the resulting \code{Embedding}.

\textbf{Run fragment workflows concurrently.}
Each fragment's ROHF basis and transformed integrals come from the captured
\code{Fragment} record, which circuit construction and SQD share, so the
preparation stage only builds and compiles circuits. It runs on HPC and stores
them as a \code{FragmentCircuits} record; a QPU gateway loads it, submits the
circuits, and stores the counts. Because QPY loads native ffsim gates as opaque
gates, the stored circuits must be compiled for the backend, or, on QRMI's
Slurm path below, decomposed into standard gates. The gateway's Qiskit must be
at least as new as the preparing node's. SQD can start as soon as that
fragment's counts arrive, although a shared QPU may serialize sampling.
Reserve the multi-node solve allocation only after the counts are available.
Short circuit-preparation stages can be bundled to reduce queue overhead.

\textbf{Use SQD's collective interface within each fragment.}
Rank 0 loads the fragment's \code{SQDState}, which holds the carryover strings
and their basis, and calls \code{FragmentSQD.prepare} with the embedding's AO
overlap, which carryover requires. It maps the carryover
onto the fragment's sampling basis and resolves the SQD inputs
(\code{SQDInputs}). Broadcast the integrals, counts, numerical options, included
configurations, and starting random-generator state so all ranks enter SQD with
agreeing arguments. Every rank calls \code{SQDInputs.run}, which calls
\code{diagonalize_fermionic_hamiltonian} with the trim policy. Within each
recovery iteration:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item SQD performs recovery on rank 0. \code{TrimPolicy} draws one pool of
  $\code{samples_per_batch} \times B_f$ bitstrings with
  \code{partition_subsample} and deals it into $B_f$ disjoint batches. SQD
  broadcasts the ordered list of batches to all ranks.
  \item \emph{Screening call.} All ranks enter the custom \code{sci_solver}
  with the $B_f$ batches. Rank $r$ solves batches $b \equiv r \pmod{P_f}$
  locally, using Fulqrum or PySCF and the threads assigned to its node; rank 0
  also solves its own. Each rank owns its numerical solver objects. Each rank
  then trims its own results, keeping the CI strings that \code{TrimPolicy}
  would keep, and returns an \code{SCIResult} whose \code{carryover} field holds
  them and whose \code{sci_state} is \code{None}. The adapter gathers these
  lightweight results on rank 0 in the original batch order; the screening
  states never leave their rank. Other ranks may return an empty list: SQD
  documents that only the control process's results are consumed.
  \item On rank 0, \code{TrimPolicy.refine} merges the trimmed strings,
  preserving each batch's ranking. SQD applies the included configurations,
  spin symmetrization and \code{max_dim}, then broadcasts the merged subspace
  together with the decision that another round follows.
  \item \emph{Merged call.} All ranks enter \code{sci_solver} again, with the
  single merged subspace. With a node-local solver, rank 0 solves it while the
  other ranks return at once and wait in the next broadcast; a distributed
  solver can use all $P_f$ ranks. Rank 0 returns the full state, because both
  carryover selection and the fragment RDMs need it.
  \item SQD invokes the callback on rank 0 with the merged result only, reports
  that result, and checks convergence. It then selects the carryover from the
  merged amplitudes using \code{carryover_threshold}, \code{carryover_ratio}
  and \code{max_carryover}. Finally it broadcasts the iteration state,
  including the best-so-far and current merged \code{SCIResult} objects, to
  every rank.
\end{enumerate}
The adapter has to tell the two calls apart. SQD documents that
\code{TrimPolicy} calls the solver twice per iteration, first with every batch
and then with the single merged subspace, so the adapter alternates by call.
The number of subspaces cannot decide this, because \code{num_batches=1} also
passes one subspace to the screening call.

The local trim must select exactly what \code{TrimPolicy.refine} would select
from the full state: SQD's marginal-weight ranking with \code{trim_ratio} and
\code{max_strings_per_trim}, implemented by the private function
\code{_select_carryover}. Pin the SQD revision and test the equality. The
\code{carryover} field and a \code{None} state are public since
qiskit-addon-sqd 0.14, and \code{TrimPolicy} uses a solver-supplied carryover as
given.

With the default \code{StandardPolicy} the adapter must instead gather full
states, because that policy selects the carryover from the lowest-energy
batch's amplitudes. Section~\ref{sec:resources} quantifies the difference.

The gathers and broadcasts synchronize successive rounds and iterations.
Fragments advance independently; a slow batch delays its own fragment's
merged round. The final best result is available on every rank. Only rank 0
calls \code{FragmentSQD.finalize}, which extracts the carryover, builds and
rotates the RDMs, and returns the \code{FragmentResult} and the next
\code{SQDState}. Only rank 0 writes the checkpoint and manifest. SQD guards its
own callback; the worker must guard \code{finalize} and the writes.

Each fragment returns energy including the constant Hamiltonian term,
spin-resolved 1-RDMs of shape $(2,n_f,n_f)$, and a spin-summed 2-RDM of shape
$(n_f,n_f,n_f,n_f)$. The RDMs must use the original LAS fragment basis.
Store the \code{FragmentResult} and \code{SQDState} records under references;
CI vectors need not pass through the Prefect coordinator.

\textbf{Replay mrh and commit the orbital update.}
Wait for all required fragment results, then call \code{update_orbitals}
with the captured \code{Embedding}. It checks each result's fragment digest and
re-enters mrh with callbacks that validate each requested Hamiltonian against
the captured one and return the stored energy and RDMs. It passes the
\emph{original starting RDMs} to the kernel; the callbacks introduce the new
RDMs at the solve boundary. It uses \code{max_cycle_macro=1} and
\code{max_cycle_rdmjk=0}. mrh computes the LAS energy from the RDMs and only
logs the fragment energies.

mrh repeats global preparation and visits the callbacks sequentially, but no
SQD solve is repeated. It then performs the coupled energy calculation, orbital
update, and canonicalization. Commit the returned orbitals and RDMs together
in their consistent basis. Embedded fragment energies are not simply additive:
the total LAS energy includes interfragment contributions.

\textbf{Launch jobs through QCSC.}
A LASSQD worker executable reads a stage request and writes a validated result
manifest. The flow invokes it through \code{run_job_from_blocks}, using
\code{CommandBlock} for the executable, \code{ExecutionProfileBlock} for
resources, and \code{HPCProfileBlock} for site configuration. Schedule capture,
fragment preparation, and replay as classical HPC stages. For each collective
fragment solve set \code{num_nodes} to $P_f$, \code{mpiprocs=1}, an MPI-capable
launcher, and appropriate \code{ompthreads} and BLAS/OpenMP environment
settings. \code{ompthreads} renders only Slurm's \code{--cpus-per-task}, and
the job script exports nothing but the profile's \code{environments}, so set
\code{OMP_NUM_THREADS} and the BLAS equivalents there explicitly and keep them
consistent with \code{ompthreads}. Configure the site's launcher and placement
options explicitly.

On Slurm, \code{num_nodes} and \code{mpiprocs} become \code{--nodes} and
\code{--ntasks-per-node}, so the task count follows $P_f$. Leave
\code{HPCProfileBlock.slurm_ntasks} unset: it is a profile-wide field and would
apply to every fragment. Fragments with different $P_f$ differ only in their
execution profile. \code{run_job_from_blocks} accepts per-call
\code{execution_profile_overrides}, but \code{submit_job_from_blocks} and the
bulk runner do not. Give them one \code{ExecutionProfileBlock} per distinct
$P_f$ instead, or a per-job execution profile in each bulk job specification. QCSC's
Slurm script starts with a non-login \texttt{\#!/bin/bash}, so a site whose
\code{module} command needs a login shell must set it up in
\code{pre_commands}. On Fugaku the PJM and launcher MPI options must express
the same rank layout.

At startup verify that there are $P_f$ ranks on $P_f$ distinct nodes and that
SQD detects the same MPI rank and size as \code{MPI.COMM_WORLD}. SQD detects
MPI at import time from launcher environment variables. One of them is
\code{SLURM_NTASKS}, so whenever Slurm sets it, a process that has mpi4py
installed and imports SQD initializes MPI, even as a single task. Each
fragment gets its own MPI world; its ranks all participate in the same SQD
call. Make MPI calls from the main thread, with an appropriate MPI thread
support level, while the local numerical libraries use node-local threads.

Use the logical job keys of Section~\ref{sec:identity} as QCSC job keys, with
QCSC's registry-backed submit/monitor APIs for restartable scheduling.
\code{run_jobs_from_blocks_bulk} can manage a pool of complete fragment MPI
jobs; individual diagonalization batches remain inside their fragment
allocation. Fugaku's \code{GlobalFugakuBulkRunner} supports rolling progress as
fragment counts arrive. QCSC provides a queue probe only for Fugaku, so bulk
execution on Slurm or PBS needs an application-supplied probe. A probe that
raises reports no free slots, so submission stalls without an error. Bulk
monitoring on Slurm queries \code{sacct}, so it needs Slurm accounting. Bound
both active fragment jobs and the total reserved nodes $\sum_f P_f$: QCSC's
bulk limits count jobs, and a job-count limit alone does not impose a node
budget.
The simple \code{run_job_from_blocks} path remains useful for initial local and
scheduler validation. The submitter needs scheduler access and shared storage
or explicit file staging.

The gateway awaits QCSC's asynchronous \code{QCSCSamplerV2} submission and
fetching, saving provider job references and counts durably. \code{run_lassqd}
is synchronous, so the gateway calls the stage functions on the stored
\code{FragmentCircuits} instead. The \code{groups} chosen at preparation decide
which fragments share a circuit, \code{submit_circuits} can submit any subset
of the circuits, and \code{collect_samples} combines pub results from several
jobs, including jobs recovered by identifier. Fragments can therefore obtain
counts independently where useful.

\textbf{Choose a QPU access path.} QCSC~0.3.1 offers two.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{QCSCSamplerV2} submits to IBM Qiskit Runtime directly from the
  gateway, through a \code{QiskitRuntimeConfig} block. It has split submit and
  fetch tasks, optional result caching, and job tags through the sampler
  options. It needs no QRMI.
  \item \code{QrmiRuntime} samples through
  QRMI.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/qiskit-community/qrmi}} With
  \code{scheduler="slurm"}, each call submits a Slurm job that requests the
  QRMI resource as its QPU. The compute node acquires the backend
  \code{Target}, transpiles and samples within a QRMI session. With
  \code{scheduler="iqp"}, new in 0.3.1, the workflow process submits one job
  directly, without a session. \code{QrmiRuntime} accepts several pubs of the
  form \code{(circuit,)} sharing one shot count, and returns a
  \code{PrimitiveResult} that \code{collect_samples} accepts. It rejects every
  option except shots, so job tags are unavailable. It keeps no cache, does
  not retry, and records the Slurm job identifier only after monitoring
  returns, so the reconciliation of Section~\ref{sec:ambiguous} must treat an
  interrupted sampler call as ambiguous.
\end{itemize}
On the Slurm path the \code{Target} exists only on the compute node, so the
preparation stage cannot compile for it. QCSC therefore splits compilation
between two application hooks, and the device mapping belongs to the
application rather than to QRMI:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{prepare} runs on the workflow host and must leave a circuit that
  QPY can carry to a node where ffsim is not installed, so it removes the
  native ffsim gates without mapping onto a device. QCSC's own SQD workflow
  runs \code{ffsim.qiskit.PRE_INIT} and then decomposes the remaining
  \code{ffsim.*} operations in a bounded loop; a generic pass manager with
  \code{PRE_INIT} as its pre-init stage and basis gates but no coupling map
  should reach the same result through basis translation, which is worth
  testing before relying on it.
  \item \code{transpiler} is an importable \code{"module:function"} that QCSC
  calls with \code{(circuit, target, config)} where the \code{Target} is
  available: on the compute node for \code{scheduler="slurm"}, and in the
  workflow process for \code{scheduler="iqp"}. Layout, routing and ISA
  compilation all happen there, and QCSC validates the result against the
  \code{Target}. \code{compile.py} supplies a preset-pass-manager default.
\end{itemize}
Only JSON crosses that boundary, so \code{config} holds portable preset
options and \code{initial_layout} travels as a list of physical qubit indices;
no \code{Target}, \code{Layout} or other Python object can. A workflow that
compiles for the backend in its preparation stage should therefore keep that
step in a function of the same three arguments, so adopting the Slurm path
means naming it instead of calling it. QRMI is needed only where the site
schedules QPU access through it.

\textbf{Persist enough state to restart.}
Requests identify the parent snapshot, fragment, Hamiltonian, basis, acquisition,
and numerical settings. Save the counts as \code{FragmentSamples} and each
fragment's carryover strings, their basis and full random-generator state as
\code{SQDState}. Sampling and each fragment use independent random streams,
spawned from one seed: \code{SQDState.from_seed} spawns a child stream from a
generator, and each fragment's stream then continues only in its own
\code{SQDState}. Concurrent fragments therefore draw the same numbers as in the
serial reference, whatever the order of their solves. During collective SQD,
rank 0 owns the
advancing recovery RNG; its saved state is authoritative. Give any stochastic
local eigensolver a seed tied to the logical batch, not completion order.

Treat a failed collective fragment solve as one failed MPI job. Propagate
errors or abort the MPI world so peers cannot remain blocked in a collective,
including failures in rank-0 preparation, callbacks, and finalization. For
example, SQD raises \code{ValueError} on rank 0 when no sample has the
requested electron counts, while the other ranks wait in the batch broadcast.

Trim SQD adds a failure on other ranks. Its pool of bitstrings is dealt among
the $B_f$ batches, so with fewer distinct bitstrings than $B_f$ some batches
are empty. qiskit-addon-sqd 0.14.0 and 0.14.1 then fail in the built-in batch
solver. The failure occurs on whichever rank owns the batch, possibly not
rank 0, while the others wait in the gather. Rank 0 should check the
postselected counts before entering SQD, and the adapter must abort the world
on any failure.

The initial restart contract reruns the whole fragment call from its input
checkpoint and saved counts; the current SQD API does not expose complete
mid-iteration continuation state. Preserve completed results from other fragments.
Commit a new global state only after replay and the orbital update succeed.
Section~\ref{sec:identity} defines how jobs are identified, when a result is
accepted, and how ambiguous submissions are recovered.

\section{Job identity, completion, and recovery}
\label{sec:identity}

\subsection{Logical job identity}

A \emph{logical job} is one stage execution for one input state. Its key
contains:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the calculation identifier;
  \item the parent snapshot: a digest of the committed orbitals, fragment RDMs,
  and fragment continuation states, with the macrocycle index for readability;
  \item the stage: \code{capture}, \code{prepare}, \code{sample}, \code{solve},
  or \code{replay};
  \item the fragment index, or \code{all} for global stages;
  \item the request digest: a hash of the canonical request record, including
  digests of its input arrays, numerical settings including the subspace
  policy and its parameters, starting random-generator states, $P_f$, and code
  and environment versions.
\end{itemize}
A readable form is
\code{<calc>/c007-<snapshot8>/solve/f03}, plus a 12-character digest of the full
key, such as \code{lq-3f9a0c71b2de}, for places with length limits. The
macrocycle index alone is insufficient: after a rollback, or a rerun with
changed settings, the same index refers to a different input. The request
digest ensures that recovery never adopts a job submitted with other inputs.
Attempts are not part of the key; each attempt gets an attempt identifier
recorded in its claim and manifest.

The key determines the job's working directory and is used as the QCSC
\code{job_key}. Its short digest is written wherever a running job can be found
again: the scheduler job name and the QPU job's tags. QCSC's Slurm and PBS
scripts set no job name, so the scheduler uses the script name, which
\code{submit_job_from_blocks} derives from the job key, for example
\code{<key>.slurm}. \code{run_job_from_blocks} takes its script name from the
caller. In QCSC~0.3.1 it also accepts a per-call \code{submit_env}, which could
pass \code{SBATCH_JOB_NAME}; verify this on the target site before relying on it.
Fugaku needs an explicit PJM job name: \code{run_job_from_blocks} accepts one,
but \code{submit_job_from_blocks} does not currently pass it. Job tags can be
set through \code{QCSCSamplerV2}'s sampler options, but not through
\code{QrmiRuntime}, which accepts only the shot count.

\subsection{Accepting a result}

The scheduler state, the QCSC registry, and the Prefect task state are evidence
about a job's execution. The \emph{result manifest} is the only evidence of
its output. The awaiting call yields that evidence rather than raising on it:
\code{run_job_from_blocks} returns a result carrying the process
\code{exit_status} and the final scheduler state, so a task that does not
inspect both treats a failed job as a successful one. A manifest is
\emph{valid} when:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item it was published atomically by rank 0 as the job's final write (written
  to a temporary name, flushed, then linked into place without replacing an
  existing manifest), after every referenced file was complete;
  \item its logical key and request digest equal those of the current request;
  \item every referenced file matches the size and SHA-256 recorded in the
  manifest;
  \item its arrays pass semantic checks: expected shapes and dtypes, finite
  values, Hermitian 1-RDMs with traces $n_\alpha$ and $n_\beta$, the 2-RDM
  partial trace $\sum_r \Gamma_{pqrr} = (N-1)\gamma_{pq}$, and an energy
  recomputed from the stored Hamiltonian and RDMs that matches the reported
  energy to a set tolerance.
\end{enumerate}
These checks cost $O(n_f^4)$ per fragment and need no rerun.

\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}llL@{}}

A valid result may therefore be accepted when the scheduler or control-side
status is \code{FAILED} or \code{UNKNOWN}. Nothing writes to a job's outputs after
publication, and the hashes and semantic checks verify them, so a later
failure cannot have altered them. Such failures include MPI finalization
errors, a nonzero exit on another rank after SQD returned, epilogue failures,
walltime expiry during cleanup, and lost scheduler accounting. The Prefect task
state is not authoritative either: if the coordinator crashes while a solve task
awaits its job, the job continues. On restart the flow derives every stage's
state from storage and the registry, not from earlier task states.

QCSC's registry already marks a job \code{SUCCEEDED} once all of its
\code{expected_outputs} exist, including records previously marked
\code{FAILED} or \code{UNKNOWN}, and never downgrades a \code{SUCCEEDED} record.
Slurm's \code{COMPLETED} state also maps to \code{SUCCEEDED} without checking
outputs. LASSQD therefore lists only the manifest as an expected output, never
the data files it references, and validates the manifest itself after QCSC
reports success. If validation fails, it moves the manifest aside so the
registry cannot promote it again, then resets the record with
\code{reset_jobs_for_rerun(statuses=[SUCCEEDED])}.

Manifests are published without replacement, so at most one manifest per
logical job is accepted. If two attempts run, the first to publish wins; the
other's outputs are discarded. For HPC stages this costs only node-hours,
because their outputs are deterministic functions of the request. For QPU
sampling it matters more: two sampler jobs give different counts. The sample
manifest therefore records the chosen provider job before any downstream stage
consumes the counts, and results from any other job for the same key are
ignored.

\subsection{Recovering from an ambiguous submission}
\label{sec:ambiguous}

A submission is ambiguous when the scheduler or QPU service may have accepted
it without the workflow recording its job identifier: the submit command timed
out or lost its connection, or the coordinator crashed between submission and
the registry update. QCSC records the scheduler job identifier only after the
submit command returns. It classifies submit errors containing ``timeout'' or
``connection'' as temporary and marks the job \code{SUBMIT_DEFERRED}, which the
bulk refill loop later submits again. For queue-full errors this is safe; for a
timeout the original submission may have succeeded. For LASSQD's multi-node
solves, treat temporary submit errors other than a full queue as ambiguous:
submit them through LASSQD code that reconciles first, rather than returning
them to the refill pool. QCSC runs \code{sbatch} and the other scheduler
commands without a timeout of its own, so a submit command that hangs blocks
its caller; impose a timeout in LASSQD's submit path and treat its expiry as
ambiguous.

Each job's rank 0 writes a \emph{claim} record at startup, using exclusive file
creation. The claim holds the logical key, request digest, attempt identifier,
the scheduler job identifier from the job's environment (\code{SLURM_JOB_ID},
\code{PJM_JOBID}, or \code{PBS_JOBID}), and the start time. A claim proves that
a job started even when its submit response was lost.

Before any submission or resubmission of a logical job, reconcile in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If a valid manifest exists, apply the acceptance rule above.
  \item If the registry holds a scheduler or provider job identifier, monitor
  that job.
  \item Otherwise search for a matching job: claim records in the job's
  directory, then the scheduler's queue and accounting by job name, or the QPU
  service by job tag. These searches cover jobs that were accepted but have not
  started.
  \item If exactly one job matches, record its identifier in the registry and
  monitor it. If several match, keep the most advanced and cancel queued
  duplicates.
  \item If none matches, do not resubmit automatically. Mark the logical job
  \code{AMBIGUOUS} and stop its branch of the workflow until an operator
  decides. An approved resubmission uses a new attempt identifier; no-replace
  publication still accepts at most one result.
\end{enumerate}
QCSC exposes no search for existing jobs, so LASSQD implements steps 3 and 4
with scheduler commands and the QPU service's job listing. QCSC's cached submit
task returns a stored QPU job reference only if the submit task completed and
persisted its result, so it does not cover a crash after the provider accepted
the job. \code{QrmiRuntime} has no cache at all.

\section{Resource limits and mitigations}
\label{sec:resources}

\subsection{Sizes of \code{sci_solver} inputs and outputs}

For fragment $f$ with $n = n_f$ orbitals, screening batch $b$ has
$d_{\alpha,b}$ alpha and $d_{\beta,b}$ beta strings and dimension $D_b =
d_{\alpha,b} d_{\beta,b}$. Under trim SQD the $B_f$ batches together draw one
pool of $\code{samples_per_batch} \times B_f$ bitstrings, and no bitstring
appears in two batches. Each spin sector of a batch holds at most the distinct
strings from \code{samples_per_batch} samples (twice that with
\code{symmetrize_spin}), plus included and carryover strings, and never more
than $\binom{n}{n_\sigma}$. Screening keeps $t_{\sigma,b} =
\min(\lceil \code{trim_ratio}\, d_{\sigma,b} \rceil, \code{max_strings_per_trim})$
strings per sector.

The merged subspace has $d_{m,\sigma} \le \min(\sum_b t_{\sigma,b} +
i_\sigma, \code{max_dim_}\sigma)$ strings, where $i_\sigma$ counts the
included strings, and dimension $D_m = d_{m,\alpha} d_{m,\beta}$. The
\code{max_dim} cap applies to every subspace after strings are merged, so
$D_b, D_m \le \code{max_dim_a} \times \code{max_dim_b}$ is the control that
bounds memory. The notebook's configuration caps both at $150^2 = 22{,}500$.
Because trimmed batches have little in common, $\sum_b t_{\sigma,b}$ is close
to $B_f$ times one batch's survivors, so \code{max_dim} rather than
\code{max_strings_per_trim} bounds $D_m$. Sizes below assume float64
amplitudes; complex amplitudes double them.

\begin{itemize}

\end{itemize}

sufficient for memory, provided the merged subspace also fits on rank 0's node
or is solved by a distributed solver.

\textbf{Idle ranks in the merged round.} With a node-local solver, the merged
diagonalization runs on rank 0 while the other $P_f - 1$ nodes wait. For
screening and merged solve times $T_b$ and $T_m$, an iteration takes about
$\lceil B_f/P_f \rceil T_b + T_m$ and keeps
\[
  \eta = \frac{B_f T_b + T_m}{P_f\,(\lceil B_f/P_f \rceil T_b + T_m)}
\]
of its node time busy. With $T_b = T_m$ and $B_f = 16$, $\eta$ is 53\,\% at
$P_f = 16$ (iteration time $2T$), 71\,\% at $P_f = 8$ ($3T$), and 85\,\% at
$P_f = 4$ ($5T$). The notebook's $B_f = 5$ on five ranks gives 60\,\%. Choose
$P_f$ from the measured $T_b$ and $T_m$ and from the cost of a node-hour
against wall time. A distributed solver for the merged call alone removes the
idle time.

\textbf{Input-to-output scaling.} A batch's input is its strings,
$O(d_\alpha + d_\beta) = O(\sqrt{D_b})$; its output state is $O(D_b)$. For
$d_\alpha = d_\beta = 10^4$, 160\,kB of strings yield an 800\,MB state. Per
iteration, the broadcasts of the screening batches and of the merged subspace
grow as $O(B_f\sqrt{D})$. With local trim, so does the gather of screening
results, and the only $O(D)$ communication is the iteration-state broadcast,
independent of $B_f$. Gathering full screening states instead grows as
$O(B_f D)$, and that gather dominates communication and rank-0 memory. Fragment
outputs and the global stages grow as $n_f^4$ independently of $D$: a 2-RDM
takes 50\,MB at $n_f = 50$ and 0.8\,GB at $n_f = 100$. Replay holds every
fragment's integrals and RDMs, $\sum_f 16 n_f^4$ bytes, in addition to mrh's own
integral intermediates, which have not been measured here.

\textbf{Minimum and maximum sizes.} The adapter must accept any subspace with
$D \ge 1$. The notebook's Fulqrum adapter handles $D = 1$ directly, because
SciPy's \code{eigsh} rejects a $1 \times 1$ linear operator. Fulqrum 0.5.0's
solver factory passes every subspace to its eigensolver, so wrap it for this
case. Counts with no bitstring of the requested electron counts stop SQD on
rank 0 before the first broadcast. Fewer distinct bitstrings than $B_f$ leave
screening batches empty. Either must abort the MPI world, as described above.

The solver receives $B_f$ subspaces in the screening call and one in the
merged call. With $P_f \le B_f$ ranks, subspace $i$ goes to rank $i \bmod
P_f$, so the merged subspace goes to rank 0 unless a distributed solver takes
it. Assigning subspaces to ranks is collective, and SQD guarantees only that
the subspace count agrees across ranks, so base the assignment on that count
and the call parity.

No interface limits the maximum size; the limits are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Memory}, as tabulated. Rank 0 is the binding constraint: through
  the merged subspace with local trim, or through the gathered states without
  it.
  \item \emph{Orbital count.} SQD switches to Python integers for 64 or more
  orbitals, but PySCF's selected CI uses 64-bit integer strings, limiting that
  path to $n_f \le 63$. LASSQD's carryover mapping also uses 64-bit strings
  and rejects 64 or more orbitals.
  \item \emph{Message size.} SQD broadcasts with mpi4py's pickle-based
  \code{bcast}. Messages above 2\,GiB fail unless both mpi4py and the MPI library
  support large counts; the iteration-state broadcast reaches that size at
  $D_m \approx 1.3 \times 10^8$. The adapter's own gather can use buffer-based
  \code{Gatherv} or \code{mpi4py.util.pkl5} instead. Test both at the target
  $D$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Consistency between inputs and outputs.} All ranks must call SQD with
identical arguments, including the policy and its parameters. At startup each
rank computes a digest of its integrals, counts, options, included
configurations, and random-generator state, and the job aborts if the digests
differ. The adapter returns results in subspace order. \code{SCIResult}
states and trimmed strings remain in the fragment ROHF basis, while the
returned RDMs are in the LAS basis. Each trimmed carryover is ordered by
descending marginal weight, as SQD requires, while the input strings arrive
sorted in ascending order. Every stored array records its basis and the digest
of the Hamiltonian it belongs to, and each manifest records the digests of its
inputs. \code{update_orbitals} rejects a \code{FragmentResult} whose fragment
digest differs from the captured fragment's.

\section{Implementation outline}
\label{sec:implementation}

The serial library provides every stage and record the workflow needs
(Section~\ref{sec:library}). What remains is an optional \code{lassqd.distributed} subpackage
of this repository, with QCSC and MPI dependencies provided as extras. The
inspected upstream libraries need no source changes; the optional mrh
optimization is a separate follow-up.

The subpackage splits into a numerical part and an orchestration part, which are
at different stages. The collective \code{sci_solver} adapter below, and the
durability layer of claim records, semantic manifest validation and
reconciliation, are unimplemented. The orchestration itself has a worked
reference at \path{examples/hchain_lassqd_prefect/} in this repository: the task
graph of Section~\ref{sec:tasks}, the record-based stage boundary, QCSC
block-driven submission with per-stage result manifests, and a serial reference
that it reproduces bitwise. That example runs at $P_f = 1$, where the library's
existing \code{prepare}/\code{run}/\code{finalize} split is already the
per-fragment worker, so it establishes steps S2 and S5 of
Section~\ref{sec:validation} on a Slurm target and leaves S3 and a non-trivial
S4 to the adapter.

The subpackage's numerical part is the collective \code{sci_solver} adapter,
which wraps a local batch solver and is aware of the trim schedule:
\begin{itemize}
  \item It alternates between the screening and the merged call.
  \item In the screening call it checks $P_f \le B_f$ and solves batches
  $b \equiv r \pmod{P_f}$ on rank $r$. It trims each result locally with
  \code{TrimPolicy}'s parameters and gathers only the trimmed strings, with
  energies and occupancies, on rank 0 in batch order.
  \item In the merged call it solves the one subspace on rank 0 and returns its
  full state there.
\end{itemize}
It preserves the library's energy, spin, determinant-ordering, and RDM
conventions. The local solver used directly as \code{sci_solver} provides the
serial numerical reference. Keep callbacks cheap: they run on rank 0 between
iterations while the other ranks wait. Both the local solves and the collective
result aggregation must fit their assigned resources.

Fulqrum 0.5.0 provides a local batch solver, the factory
\code{sci_solver_from_integrals} in \code{fulqrum.addon_sqd_support}. It retains the
transpose between Fulqrum's vector ordering and SQD's alpha-by-beta amplitude
matrix. It relies on the ascending CI-string order that SQD now documents, and
reuses one \code{SubspaceHamiltonian} across calls. It binds the integrals at
construction and checks those passed on each call against them, while each
macrocycle changes a fragment's integrals. Build one per fragment solve, from
the captured \code{Fragment}'s \code{h1} and \code{h2}, and reuse it for both
rounds of every iteration. Its checks raise rather than abort, so the adapter
must convert any exception into an MPI abort. Fulqrum's source notes that
these checks will need to abort collectively once Fulqrum supports MPI.

The rest of the subpackage is orchestration. It defines versioned request,
claim, and manifest records that reference the library's HDF5 records and
their digests. It also holds the Prefect flows, worker entry points, the QPU
gateway, storage adapters, MPI resource profiles, reconciliation, and
failure/retry handling. The example cited above implements the flows, the
worker entry points, the QPU gateway and a size-and-digest manifest, and
documents which of the remaining records and rules it omits. Co-installation
with the inspected QCSC packages requires Python 3.11 or newer; separate
gateway and worker environments can communicate through the records. The MPI
worker environment also needs \code{mpi4py} built for the site's MPI runtime.
LASSQD requires \code{qiskit-addon-sqd>=0.14.0}, which adds trim SQD, subspace
policies and solver-selected carryover to the MPI collective module of 0.13.1;
confirm that the deployed revision behaves as described here. SQD's main
branch, after 0.14.1, requires Python 3.11. QCSC's \code{QrmiRuntime} imports
Prefect~3 modules, although the package declares \code{prefect>=2.19}. In
QCSC~0.3.1, \code{qcsc-prefect-dice} pins \code{qiskit-addon-sqd==0.14.0}, and
the new \code{qcsc-prefect-fulqrum} requires at least 0.14.0; both are
compatible with LASSQD.

The new \code{qcsc-prefect-fulqrum} and \code{qcsc-prefect-sbd} packages run
Fulqrum and SBD diagonalizations as separate scheduler jobs. Fulqrum's runs
include a one-rank-per-GPU MPI backend, which returns carryover strings
without amplitudes. These solvers manage their own processes, so they fit
SQD's non-collective solver mode rather than the collective fragment job
above, and they pay a queue wait per diagonalization. They could serve the
screening calls, which need only strings. They cannot serve the merged call
unchanged, because LASSQD builds the fragment RDMs from the merged state.

SQD detects MPI when it is imported, so capture and replay workers launched
with several ranks must not import qiskit-addon-sqd. Importing
\code{initial_state}, \code{capture_embedding}, \code{update_orbitals} and the
records does not, and the library's tests check this.

Control BLAS/OpenMP threads within each node and verify rank placement.
Global capture and replay use mrh's numerical execution; provision a
suitable shared-memory node for their integrals and orbital calculations.
Retaining fragment allocations avoids queueing between recovery iterations, but
nodes wait during rank-0 recovery, merging, diagnostics, and the merged solve,
and for the slowest batch in each screening round. Measure repeated preparation, queue time,
local solves, communication, rank-0 processing, orbital work, and peak memory
on every rank before claiming an optimal node count or speedup.

\section{Numerical validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Define the numerical validation before implementing the distributed workflow.
The distributed workflow changes how and where the library's stages run, not
what they compute, and each step below changes one aspect of execution, so a
difference can be attributed to it. Workflow validation and numerical
equivalence are separate tasks with separate tests.

\subsection{Step-by-step comparison}

Each step introduces one change and is compared only with its predecessor, with
a stated expectation. ``Bitwise'' agreement assumes the same libraries, thread
counts, and hardware, and deterministic eigensolver start vectors; the
notebook's adapter sets \code{v0}, whereas ARPACK's default start is random.
Where bitwise agreement is not achievable, use tight tolerances on invariant
quantities.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lLL@{}}
\toprule
Step & Change from the previous step & Expected agreement \\
\midrule
S1 & Serial \code{run_lassqd} with \code{TrimPolicy} and the serial batch
solver, in the notebook's configuration, instrumented to record traces. &
Baseline. \\
S2 & The stages as separate processes, each reading and writing the stored
records, replaying the counts stored at S1. & Bitwise with S1. \\
S3 & Collective adapter with local trim on one machine: 1 rank, then
$P_f = B_f$ ranks, then a $P_f$ that does not divide $B_f$. & Identical
screening batches, trimmed strings, merged subspaces, carryover strings, and
final RNG state; energies and RDMs bitwise with equal thread counts. \\
S4 & Target scheduler, one rank per node. & As S3, within tolerance if node
libraries differ. \\
S5 & Prefect/QCSC orchestration, replaying the counts stored at S4. & Bitwise
with S4. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The 1-rank case of S3 also checks that the adapter's local trim selects the
same strings as \code{TrimPolicy} does from full states.

S2 and S5 replay stored counts because no record holds the sampler's
random-generator state. Each fragment solver's stream lives in its own
\code{SQDState}, but \code{run_lassqd} holds one sampler object whose stream
advances across macrocycles and across the pubs of one job, and nothing
persists it. A distributed workflow that samples afresh from a simulator
therefore cannot reproduce a serial reference that did the same, unless both
derive each macrocycle's sampler stream from the seed and the macrocycle index
rather than from a live sampler object. Sampling on a QPU makes the question
moot: the counts differ between jobs, and the stored \code{FragmentSamples} fix
them for everything downstream.

The library builds fragment problems from the alpha one-electron integrals
\code{h1s[0]} and stops on the energy change between macrocycles; the
distributed workflow keeps both. Full spin-dependent embedding and stronger
convergence criteria are separate numerical changes.

Two configurations isolate numerical changes from randomness:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{RNG-insensitive SQD.} Use one recovery iteration, no initial
  occupancies, \code{num_batches=1}, and no binding \code{max_dim}, and set
  \code{samples_per_batch} to at least the number of postselected bitstrings.
  \code{TrimPolicy} still shuffles its pool, but the single batch then holds
  every postselected bitstring in some order, and the screening subspace,
  trim and merged subspace do not depend on that order. Under
  \code{StandardPolicy} the same settings copy all bitstrings into every batch
  without random draws. Stored counts fix the samples. Differences then come
  only from bases, Hamiltonians, initialization, or execution.
  \item \emph{Exact limit.} Use \code{trim_ratio=1} and no binding
  \code{max_dim}, so that the merged subspace is the union of the batches'
  strings in each spin sector. When the counts cover each fragment's full
  space, SQD is then exact and independent of randomness and batch partition.
  Results can be compared with classical LASSCF using exact fragment solvers,
  as in the library's tests.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Intermediate comparison points}

Every step records a trace of the following quantities, keyed by macrocycle,
fragment, iteration, round, and batch. Small arrays are stored in full; large arrays are
stored as digests with norms.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Captured Hamiltonians $(h_0, h_1, h_2)$ and their digests.
  \item Fragment ROHF bases, compared through their overlap, which must be a
  signed permutation, whether each ROHF converged, and the transformed
  integrals.
  \item Counts digests and random-generator states before and after each
  fragment solve.
  \item Per iteration: the ordered screening batch strings, per-batch energies
  and dimensions, each batch's trimmed strings, the merged subspace strings,
  the merged energy and dimension, occupancies, and carryover strings.
  \item Carryover mapping between macrocycles: the orbital overlap, the
  assignment, and the mapped strings.
  \item Fragment results: energy, 1-RDMs, and 2-RDM in the LAS basis.
  \item Global state: total energy, and orbitals compared through active-space
  projectors rather than coefficients.
\end{itemize}
Compare invariants rather than gauge-dependent values: RDMs, energies, and
projectors instead of CI vector signs or orbitals within degenerate subspaces.
A comparison tool walks two traces in execution order and reports the first
record outside tolerance, which locates where results first diverge.

\subsection{Workflow validation}

Workflow tests use a stub solver that returns deterministic arrays of the
correct shapes, QCSC's local target, and fault injection: a lost submit
response, a coordinator crash after submission, a crash on rank 0 and on
another rank, a duplicate job, a corrupted or partial manifest, and an
\code{UNKNOWN} scheduler state. They verify the identity, acceptance, and
recovery rules of Section~\ref{sec:identity}, termination without blocked
collectives, whole-fragment restart, and the absence of partial commits,
without numerical cost. Numerical tests run without Prefect.

\subsection{Reducing validation overhead}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Stage replay.} Every stage maps a stored request to a stored
  result, so a change to one stage is validated by rerunning only that stage on
  stored requests. A new collective adapter, for example, reruns fragment
  solves from stored S2 records without capture, sampling, or replay.
  \item \emph{Content-addressed reuse.} Request digests identify results. When
  a change affects one stage, only that stage and its dependents are
  recomputed; upstream results are reused.
  \item \emph{Incremental comparison.} Compare the first macrocycle's stage
  outputs before running more, and stop at the first divergence.
  \item \emph{Checkpoint equivalence.} Step S2 establishes that restoring from
  stored records is equivalent to running from scratch, which justifies
  validating segments independently.
  \item \emph{Small references in CI.} Traces from the small systems in
  \path{tests/} and \path{docs/lassqd.ipynb}, the trim SQD reference, run on
  every change; target-scale
  validation runs one or two macrocycles per release.
  \item \emph{Continuous checks in production.} The manifest's semantic checks
  validate every result as it is produced, without rerunning anything.
\end{itemize}

\section{After validation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Freeze references.} Record the validated revisions and store
  the traces as regression references.
  \item \textbf{Characterize performance at the target scale.} Choose $B_f$,
  $P_f$, threads per rank, \code{max_dim}, \code{trim_ratio}, poll intervals,
  and the node budget from measured screening and merged solve times and
  per-rank peak memory. Compare trim SQD with \code{num_batches=1} on the
  target system to confirm that screening helps.
  \item \textbf{Pilot with real hardware.} Run a few macrocycles with the QPU,
  monitor manifest checks and reconciliation events, and exercise the operator
  procedure for \code{AMBIGUOUS} jobs.
  \item \textbf{Production.} Run complete calculations. The final committed
  snapshot provides consistent orbitals and RDMs for analysis, such as LAS-PDFT
  through \code{lassqd.pdft}.
  \item \textbf{Adopt optimizations.} Introduce matrix-free operators, a
  distributed solver for the merged call, the mrh stage interfaces, and any SQD
  extension one at a time, each validated by stage replay against the frozen
  references.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Optional follow-up: changes to mrh}

The workflow above requires no mrh changes. A separate optimization can replace
capture/replay with public numerical stages:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{prepare_las_iteration} builds fragment Hamiltonians and retains
  the global integral and embedding intermediates.
  \item \code{advance_las_orbitals} consumes completed fragment RDMs, reuses
  those intermediates, and performs the orbital update without invoking
  fragment solvers.
\end{itemize}

These interfaces remove the second initial preparation pass. Integral
transformations required by changed orbitals still occur. The extension does
not change the Prefect task graph or make mrh responsible for parallel workers.
Adoption belongs after validation and profiling of the unchanged-mrh workflow;
compare its energies, orbitals, and RDMs against that capture/replay reference.
